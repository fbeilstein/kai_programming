\begin{frame}{Connections: The Big Picture}
The symbol $\frac{dy}{dx}$ sits at the intersection of:

\vspace{0.2cm}
\begin{columns}[T]
\begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Calculus
    \item Linear algebra
    \item Differential geometry
    \item Topology
    \item Dynamical systems
    \item Fourier \& Laplace transforms
    \item Differential forms
    \item de Rham cohomology
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\begin{column}{0.45\textwidth}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Probability theory
    \item Lie groups \& symmetry
    \item Complex analysis
    \item Calculus of variations
    \item Symplectic \& contact geometry
    \item Control \& systems theory
    \item Numerical analysis
    \item Physics \& engineering
\end{itemize}
\end{column}
\end{columns}

\vspace{0.4cm}

Differential equations are one of the most \textbf{interconnected} subjects in all of mathematics --- nearly every major branch either contributes tools for solving them or arose from trying to understand them.
\end{frame}


\begin{frame}{Unifying Picture}
\[
\frac{dy}{dx}
\]

can simultaneously be seen as

\begin{itemize}
\item a \textbf{ratio of infinitesimal changes} (Leibniz)
\item a \textbf{limit} (Cauchy, Weierstrass)
\item a \textbf{tangent slope} (geometry)
\item a \textbf{linear approximation} (analysis)
\item a \textbf{vector field component} (differential geometry)
\item a \textbf{differential form relation} (exterior calculus)
\item a \textbf{generator of a flow} (dynamical systems)
\item an \textbf{Euler--Lagrange condition} (calculus of variations)
\item a \textbf{Hamiltonian flow} (symplectic geometry)
\item a \textbf{Legendrian curve} in jet space (contact geometry)
\item a \textbf{multiplication} in frequency space (Fourier/Laplace)
\item a \textbf{cohomology class} obstruction (de Rham)
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.3cm}
Differential equations are one of the main bridges between
\begin{center}
\textbf{calculus \quad geometry \quad topology \quad algebra \quad physics}
\end{center}
\end{frame}
